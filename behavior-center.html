Early Childhood • Behavior Support • Social Emotional Learning

Behavior Is Communication

Children communicate through words, actions, emotions, play, and behavior. Observing what happens before and after a challenging moment can help educators better understand a child's needs.

Positive behavior support is not simply about stopping unwanted behavior. It is about building relationships, adapting environments, teaching meaningful skills, and helping children feel safe, capable, and connected.

Classroom Supports

Create a Positive Learning Environment

📅

Predictable Routines

Keep daily sequences consistent and prepare children for changes whenever possible.

🔄

Transition Supports

🧸

⭐

Clear Expectations

Model expectations positively and provide frequent opportunities to practice them.

Intentional Teaching Practices

Respond With Purpose, Not Assumptions

Teachers can use observation, collaboration, and reflective practice to select supports that fit a child's individual circumstances.

Observe First

Describe what happened objectively, including the setting, activity, and people present.

Look for Patterns

Consider what occurred before and after the behavior and whether patterns emerge over time.

Plan a Response

Adjust routines or the environment and identify a skill to teach with appropriate adult support.

Review Progress

Document responses, collaborate with the team, and adjust supports based on what helps the child.
