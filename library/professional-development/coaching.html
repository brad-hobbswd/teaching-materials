🧑‍🏫 Coaching & Mentoring Toolkit

Turn classroom observations into respectful, practical coaching conversations and manageable next steps. Use these tools for peer mentoring, instructional coaching, team reflection, and continuous improvement.

Open the Coaching Tools
A REPEATABLE COACHING CYCLE

Observe. Reflect. Plan. Follow Up.

Effective coaching is collaborative, strengths-based, specific, and connected to what children experience in the classroom. Adapt this cycle to your program's coaching model, policies, and staff roles.

1

Agree on a focus

Choose one meaningful practice or question with the educator. Clarify the purpose of the visit and how notes will be used.

2

Gather evidence

Record observable actions, educator language, environment features, and children's responses. Separate what was seen or heard from interpretation.

3

Reflect together

Start with strengths and the educator's perspective. Use open questions to explore what worked, what was difficult, and why.

4

Choose one next step

Agree on a realistic action, available support, evidence of progress, and a follow-up date. Revisit and adjust rather than treating the plan as a rating.

Important: This is an independent planning resource, not an official Head Start instrument or a substitute for your program's supervision, evaluation, confidentiality, or professional development procedures.

TOOL 1

Classroom Walkthrough & Observation Notes

Use this page to capture a brief, focused snapshot. Choose only the observation areas that match the agreed coaching focus.

Privacy reminder: do not enter children's full names, birth dates, diagnoses, or other personally identifying information into this general tool. Follow your program's recordkeeping and confidentiality policies.

TOOL 2

Professional Goal & Action Plan

Make the next step specific, realistic, supported, and observable. One focused goal is usually more useful than a long list of changes.

TOOL 3

Reflective Conversation Prompts

Use these prompts selectively. Pause, listen, and allow the educator to do most of the thinking rather than rushing to provide a solution.

Begin with strengthsWhat felt successful to you? What did you notice children doing in response?
Explore evidenceWhat did we see or hear that helps us understand this moment?
Consider accessWhose participation was supported? Who might need another way to enter the experience?
Invite possibilitiesWhat else could you try? What might happen if we changed one part of the routine?
Connect to learningWhat skill or developmental progression are we hoping to support?
Choose a next stepWhat feels manageable to try before we meet again?

Feedback sentence frames

  • Describe: “I noticed…” followed by a specific observable action.
  • Connect: “One possible effect on children's participation was…”
  • Ask: “What were you hoping children would experience in that moment?”
  • Collaborate: “Would it be useful to try… together and then reflect on what we notice?”
  • Affirm agency: “Which option feels most useful for your classroom right now?”
TOOL 4

Follow-Up & Progress Review

Review the agreed goal, recognize progress, and decide together whether to continue, adjust, or select a new focus.
