Authentic Assessment

Why Observation Matters

Observation is one of the most valuable assessment tools available to early childhood educators. By watching children during natural play, routines, and everyday experiences, teachers gain meaningful insight into each child's development, interests, strengths, and emerging skills.

1

Observe

Watch children during authentic learning experiences without interrupting their natural exploration and interactions.

2

Document

Record objective notes, meaningful quotes, photographs, and work samples that capture children's learning and development.

3

Reflect

Review observations to recognize patterns, identify developmental progress, and celebrate each child's unique growth.

4

Plan

Use authentic assessment data to design engaging experiences that support children's individual needs, interests, and next steps in learning.

Observation Methods

Choosing the Right Observation Strategy

Different observation methods provide different perspectives on children's learning and development. Using a variety of approaches gives educators a more complete picture of each child's strengths, interests, and progress.

Anecdotal Records

Brief, objective notes describing significant moments observed during everyday classroom experiences.

Running Records

Continuous observations recorded over a specific period of time to document behavior and learning.

Time Sampling

Observe and record behaviors at predetermined intervals to identify routines and developmental patterns.

Event Sampling

Record observations whenever a specific behavior or event occurs to better understand recurring situations.

Learning Stories

Narrative observations that highlight children's strengths, interests, accomplishments, and meaningful learning experiences.

Child Portfolios

Organize observations, photographs, work samples, and developmental evidence into a comprehensive record of each child's learning journey.

Observation Best Practices

Making Every Observation Meaningful

Effective observations are objective, intentional, and focused on children's authentic learning. These best practices help educators collect accurate documentation that supports planning, assessment, and meaningful family communication.

Observe Before Interpreting

Record exactly what you see and hear before making conclusions. Objective observations provide the strongest evidence for authentic assessment.

Record Children's Words

Whenever possible, write children's exact statements. Their language provides valuable insight into thinking, communication, and understanding.

Include Dates and Context

Document when and where the observation occurred along with the activity so future planning is based on meaningful evidence.

Collect Multiple Forms of Evidence

Combine anecdotal notes, photographs, children's work, and learning stories to create a complete picture of development.

Look for Growth Over Time

One observation captures a moment. Multiple observations reveal patterns, progress, strengths, and emerging skills.

Protect Confidentiality

Store documentation securely, respect children's privacy, and share information only with appropriate families and staff.

Focus on Strengths

Document what children can do while recognizing emerging skills and providing opportunities for continued growth.

Plan from Your Observations

Use observation data to guide lesson plans, classroom environments, and individualized learning experiences that meet children's interests and developmental needs.
