Build, Test, and Improve

A five-day engineering challenge that integrates oral language, early math, collaboration, planning, testing, and revision.

Open blank weekly template

Weekly planning overview

Learning objective

Children will plan and build a bridge or pathway that allows a toy to cross a small gap, test their design, and make at least one change based on what happens. Each child will share one idea, observation, or improvement.

Suggested ELOF connections

Scientific Reasoning; Mathematics Development; Approaches to Learning; Language and Communication; Social and Emotional Development; Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development. Add Literacy when children document designs, interpret informational texts, or communicate through drawing and emergent writing.

Materials

Large blocks, sturdy cardboard strips, paper tubes, stable boxes or books as supports, a small toy, paper, markers, and photos or drawings for documenting designs. Avoid sharp edges, unstable heights, and small parts.

Family partnership

Invite children to notice bridges, ramps, or pathways in their community with a family member. Ask them to describe what the structure helps people or objects do.

Planning note: This is an original planning resource, not an official Head Start form. Adjust activities to individual goals, children's interests, your approved curriculum, classroom routines, and program safety guidance. ELOF connections are suggestions; choose specific outcomes using your program's planning process.

Five-day teaching plan

Daily experiences are invitations, not rigid scripts. Follow children's questions and engagement, repeat experiences when useful, and adjust pacing as needed.

Monday | Introduce the challenge

Prepare: Two stable, low supports, a small toy, safe bridge materials, sketch paper, and markers.

Observe and document: Record the child's initial plan or explanation and what they identify as the bridge's purpose. Accept drawings, gestures, models, home language, or AAC.

Show a toy and a small gap between stable, low supports. Ask, How could we help the toy cross? Invite children to sketch, dictate, build a quick model, or explain a first idea. Think aloud about the problem without giving them a solution: We need a path that reaches both sides. What could we try? Record children's ideas so they can revisit them after testing.

Teaching focus: Invite multiple solutions. Record children's language about what a bridge needs to do, such as span a gap or hold a toy.

Tuesday | Plan and build

Prepare: Child-safe building materials, visual plan-build-test-improve cards, and optional team role cards.

Observe and document: Note how the child selects materials, communicates a plan, shares space, or participates in a team role. Record the support that helps participation.

Children work individually or in small teams to select materials and build a first design. Invite them to explain their plan before testing.

Teaching focus: Support turn taking with simple team roles. Provide a visual plan-build-test-improve sequence and accessible ways to communicate.

Wednesday | Test and observe

Prepare: Completed designs, the same lightweight test toy for each design, and a simple way to record what happens.

Observe and document: Capture what the child notices during the test and how they describe the result. Distinguish the observed outcome from their explanation of why it happened.

With an adult supervising, move the toy across each bridge. Children describe what happened and identify one part that stayed stable or changed.

Teaching focus: Treat a bridge that collapses as useful information, not failure. Ask what the group noticed before proposing changes.

Thursday | Improve the design

Prepare: The original designs, available building materials, and comparison sheets or drawing tools to capture changes.

Observe and document: Record what the child changes and the reason they give or demonstrate. Note whether they use information from the earlier test.

Children revise their bridge using evidence from the previous test. Invite them to compare the original and revised designs.

Teaching focus: Offer optional challenges such as widening the gap or comparing two supports. Keep structures low and stable.

Friday | Explain and reflect

Prepare: Children’s designs and notes, space for sharing, and drawing or dictation options for reflection.

Observe and document: Capture one design decision, one piece of evidence from testing, and one next-step idea the child communicates in any accessible mode.

Children present their bridge, explain one change they made, and share what they might try next. Document the build with drawings or approved photos.

Teaching focus: Celebrate planning, cooperation, and persistence as well as the final design. Encourage children to use evidence from testing.

Teacher prompts and differentiated pathways

Prompts for design thinking

“What does your bridge need to do?” “What happened during the test?” “Which part would you change first?” “How could we compare the two designs?” “What makes you think that change helped?”

Make teamwork accessible

Offer visual plan-build-test-improve cards, stable pre-positioned supports, larger building materials, a partner, and clear turn-taking roles. Accept explanations through speech, home language, gesture, drawings, models, or AAC.

Support persistence without taking over

When a design does not work, describe what happened neutrally and invite the child to choose a next step. Avoid building the solution for the child. Model safe testing and keep structures low and stable.

Extend learning

Children ready for more can compare spans, count supports, draw labeled plans, or graph design results. Keep comparisons fair by changing one feature at a time when practical.

Learning-center extensions

Keep the engineering cycle available for children to revisit during play. Invite children to plan, build, test, and improve without prescribing one correct design.

Blocks and engineering

Offer blocks, cardboard tubes, broad craft sticks, and picture challenge cards. Children can build ramps, shelters, or bridges, then test them with safe, lightweight objects under supervision.

Art and design studio

Provide paper, markers, and reusable drawing boards for sketches, maps, or labels. Accept dictated plans and models as well as written or drawn plans.

Math and measurement

Offer linking cubes, nonstandard measuring tools, and simple recording sheets. Children can compare lengths, count supports, or represent test results with marks or picture graphs.

Books and vocabulary

Share books about buildings, bridges, machines, and inventors. Add vocabulary cards such as balance, support, strong, longer, test, and improve for children to use during play.

Dramatic play

Create a pretend construction site with hard hats for dramatic play, clipboards, blueprints, and large safe props. Children can negotiate roles, describe a problem, and plan together.

Outdoor problem solving

Invite children to explore how to move a ball along a safe low ramp or how to create a path using large loose parts approved for the setting. Define boundaries and inspect materials before play.

Individualize: Use visual sequence cards, adapted grips, accessible work surfaces, partner roles, home-language discussion, and AAC. Keep structures low and stable, avoid sharp or small materials, and supervise every test.

Observation and documentation

Document planning, material choices, descriptive vocabulary, collaboration, counting or comparing, response to unexpected results, and the way children revise ideas. Capture children's own explanations and examples.

Adaptations and extensions

Invite children to compare bridge lengths, count blocks used, draw a labeled plan, or graph which materials were used most often. Keep extensions playful and based on observed readiness.

Health and safety

Use child-safe materials and stable, low structures. Supervise testing, remove damaged cardboard or sharp edges, and keep walkways clear.
