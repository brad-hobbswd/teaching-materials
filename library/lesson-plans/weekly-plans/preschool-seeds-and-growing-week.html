Seeds, Sprouts, and Growing

A weeklong investigation that helps children make predictions, observe change, use new vocabulary, and represent their ideas.

Open blank weekly template

Weekly planning overview

Learning objective

Children will make a prediction about what a seed needs, represent that prediction through drawing, speech, gesture, or dictation, and later describe at least one observed change.

Suggested ELOF connections

Scientific Reasoning; Language and Communication; Literacy; Approaches to Learning; Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development. Select age-appropriate preschool subdomains and goals that match children's observations, predictions, and communication.

Materials

Large classroom-approved seeds for adult-led handling, clear containers, damp paper towels or soil, water, observation sheets, crayons, picture cards, books about plants, and a designated observation location.

Family partnership

Invite families to notice a plant, tree, or seed in their neighborhood and talk about what they see. No purchase is required; children may draw or describe the observation at school.

Planning note: This is an original planning resource, not an official Head Start form. Adjust activities to individual goals, children's interests, your approved curriculum, classroom routines, and program safety guidance. ELOF connections are suggestions; choose specific outcomes using your program's planning process.

Five-day teaching plan

Daily experiences are invitations, not rigid scripts. Follow children's questions and engagement, repeat experiences when useful, and adjust pacing as needed.

Monday | Meet a seed

Prepare: A classroom-approved seed or image, a plant picture book, prediction paper, and drawing tools.

Observe and document: Capture the child's initial question or prediction in their own words, drawing, home language, gesture, or selected picture.

Show a classroom-approved seed and invite children to describe its size, shape, and color. Read a short nonfiction picture book about plants, then ask, What do you think might happen if a seed gets water? What makes you think so? Record predictions in children's own words or drawings without labeling them right or wrong. Use picture choices, dictation, or home language as needed.

Teaching focus: Record children's questions and predictions without marking them right or wrong. Children can draw, dictate, gesture, or choose picture cards.

Tuesday | Plant and predict

Prepare: Prepared containers, soil or damp paper towel, approved seeds, labels, and handwashing supplies.

Observe and document: Note the child's role in planting, the sequence they follow, and any questions about what the seed needs. Record supports offered.

Help children place seeds in prepared containers with damp paper towel or soil. Invite each child to make a drawing or dictated prediction about what may happen next.

Teaching focus: Model the words seed, root, sprout, grow, damp, and observe. Follow classroom rules for handling seeds and soil.

Wednesday | Look closely

Prepare: The labeled containers, dated observation sheets, drawing tools, and child-safe magnifiers if available.

Observe and document: Record one specific feature the child notices and whether they connect it to an earlier observation or prediction.

Revisit the containers and compare what children see with Monday's predictions. Use magnifiers if available and safe. Draw or dictate an observation.

Teaching focus: Some seeds may not show visible change yet. Encourage careful looking and patience rather than promising a specific timeline.

Thursday | Compare and care

Prepare: Plant containers, a simple care reminder, and picture vocabulary for water, light, root, and sprout.

Observe and document: Note how the child compares containers, explains a possible difference, or participates in plant care. Separate what they observe from what they infer.

Invite children to notice differences among containers and discuss what plants may need. Help them check moisture and maintain the observation area.

Teaching focus: Use prompts such as “What do you notice?” and “What might we look for tomorrow?” Do not overwater or place containers where they could fall.

Friday | Share the evidence

Prepare: Children’s earlier predictions and observations, paper or display space, and options for sharing through drawing, dictation, or speech.

Observe and document: Record how the child revisits an earlier prediction, describes evidence, or raises a new question. Value the investigation even if visible growth has not occurred.

Children share a drawing, dictated sentence, or oral explanation about one thing they observed. Revisit the original predictions and add new questions for next week.

Teaching focus: Focus on how children's thinking changed as they observed. If no sprout appears, the investigation still provides meaningful evidence and questions.

Learning-center extensions

Keep the seed investigation visible across the week so children can revisit questions, represent their ideas, and connect the study to play.

Science and discovery

Set up a supervised observation table with labeled plant containers, dated drawings, and child-safe magnifiers. Invite children to notice changes without requiring a sprout on a particular day.

Blocks and construction

Offer blocks and pictures of gardens or greenhouses. Children can build a garden, decide where containers belong, and explain how they will protect plants from being knocked over.

Dramatic play

Create a garden center with empty clean pots, picture plant labels, pretend tools, and order forms. Invite children to act as gardeners, customers, or delivery helpers.

Books and writing

Share nonfiction and storybooks about seeds and plants. Add paper, drawing tools, and dictation so children can make observation journals, signs, questions, or family notes.

Art and representation

Provide crayons, paint, and collage materials to represent a seed, root, sprout, or imagined garden. Invite children to describe their work; do not require a matching model.

Outdoor learning

Observe plants in the outdoor space without picking or tasting them. Notice sunlight, shade, soil, and changes over time, following site rules and allergy precautions.

Individualize: Offer picture vocabulary, home-language discussion, partner observation, accessible tools, and alternate ways to record ideas through drawing, dictation, pointing, or AAC. Keep seeds, soil, and small tools supervised and out of mouths.

Observation and documentation

Save dated drawings or dictated comments. Note the child's prediction, descriptive vocabulary, attention to changes, questions, and how they refer to evidence from earlier observations.

Adaptations and extensions

Compare two carefully planned conditions, such as a damp and a dry paper towel, with adult preparation and ongoing care. Invite children to record results with drawings or simple marks.

Health and safety

Prevent seeds or soil from being mouthed or swallowed. Check allergies and program guidance, wash hands after handling materials, and supervise all use of containers and tools.
