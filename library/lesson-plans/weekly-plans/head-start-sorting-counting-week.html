Sort, Count, and Compare

A five-day math and language investigation using familiar classroom objects, children's ideas, and playful problem solving.

Open blank weekly template

Weekly planning overview

Learning objective

By the end of the week, children will sort familiar objects using at least one observable attribute and explain or demonstrate their sorting rule. With appropriate support, they will count small groups using one-to-one correspondence and compare quantities.

Suggested ELOF connections

Mathematics Development; Language and Communication and Literacy, as relevant; Approaches to Learning; Social and Emotional Development; Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development. For preschoolers, use the specific Mathematics Development subdomain and goal that matches the learning objective.

Materials

Large blocks, toy animals, lids or classroom counters that are safe for the age group, sorting hoops or trays, picture category cards, number cards, paper, markers, and a simple class graph.

Family partnership

Invite children to sort socks, pantry items, or toys with a family member using safe everyday objects. Ask families to share the child's sorting rule in their preferred language.

Planning note: This is an original planning resource, not an official Head Start form. Adjust activities to individual goals, children's interests, your approved curriculum, classroom routines, and program safety guidance. ELOF connections are suggestions; choose specific outcomes using your program's planning process.

Five-day teaching plan

Daily experiences are invitations, not rigid scripts. Follow children's questions and engagement, repeat experiences when useful, and adjust pacing as needed.

Monday | Notice and sort

Invite children to inspect a collection of large blocks or toy animals. Ask what they notice, then model sorting by one feature such as color or type. Children create their own groups.

Teaching focus: Look for whether a child notices a feature, makes a category, or changes a strategy. Accept explanations through words, gestures, home language, or AAC.

Tuesday | Count each group

Children count objects in each group by moving one object at a time. Model touching or moving one object for each number word and check the final count together.

Teaching focus: Use small groups first. If a child skips or double-counts, slow down and model rather than correcting harshly.

Wednesday | More, fewer, same

Compare two groups. Invite children to predict which has more, then check by matching objects one-to-one or counting when appropriate.

Teaching focus: Use concrete questions: “How could we check?” Avoid expecting every child to know comparison vocabulary independently.

Thursday | Sort a new way

Challenge children to sort the same objects using a different attribute. Invite them to explain how one object could belong in a different group under a new rule.

Teaching focus: Notice flexible thinking and persistence. Offer visual examples and let children work with a partner if useful.

Friday | Make a class graph

Let children vote on a favorite classroom object or color. Add one mark per vote to a simple picture graph and discuss which category has more, fewer, or the same.

Teaching focus: Children may point, place a picture, dictate, or speak. Review the week's math words through the children's own examples.

Observation and documentation

Record the sorting attribute used, counting strategy, comparison language, independence, and supports needed. Capture children's exact words or actions rather than writing broad judgments such as “understands math.”

Adaptations and extensions

Offer patterns, sorting by two attributes, tally marks, or child-created categories to children who are ready. Reduce the number of objects and use picture categories for children who benefit from more support.

Health and safety

Use objects large enough to avoid choking risks, inspect materials, supervise group play, and adapt object choices to children's ages and individual needs.
