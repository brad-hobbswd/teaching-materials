Sensory Discovery

A flexible five-day plan built around responsive relationships, safe sensory exploration, movement, and early communication.

Open blank weekly template

Weekly planning overview

Learning objective

Children will explore safe sensory materials in ways that fit their development and communicate attention, curiosity, preference, or a need for a break through gaze, movement, gestures, sounds, signs, or words.

Suggested ELOF connections

Perceptual, Motor, and Physical Development; Cognition; Language and Communication; Approaches to Learning; Social and Emotional Development.

Materials

Large fabric swatches, sturdy textured balls sized for the age group, sealed sensory bottles securely assembled by adults, board books about touch, scarves, shallow water tray for closely supervised toddler use, towels, and familiar household objects that have been safety checked.

Family partnership

Invite families to share a familiar song, safe texture, or household sound that comforts or interests their child. Offer a no-cost home idea such as noticing sounds during a daily routine.

Planning note: This is an original planning resource, not an official Head Start form. Adjust activities to individual goals, children's interests, your approved curriculum, classroom routines, and program safety guidance. ELOF connections are suggestions; choose specific outcomes using your program's planning process.

Five-day teaching plan

Daily experiences are invitations, not rigid scripts. Follow children's questions and engagement, repeat experiences when useful, and adjust pacing as needed.

Monday | Textures

Offer two or three large, securely made fabric textures. Sit face-to-face, name the texture, and wait for the child's response. For infants, bring one material into view and reach; toddlers may choose between two options.

Teaching focus: Notice gaze, reaching, grasping, withdrawal, vocalization, signs, or words. Follow each child's cues and never force touching.

Tuesday | Sounds

Share a short sound-and-pause game using a rattle designed for the child's age or a gentle adult-made sound. Pause so children can anticipate, respond, or initiate.

Teaching focus: Model simple words such as loud, soft, again, and stop. Avoid sudden loud sounds and respect children who cover their ears or turn away.

Wednesday | Movement

Move a scarf slowly within an infant's comfortable field of view or invite toddlers to reach, crawl, walk, or move a scarf from one basket to another.

Teaching focus: Offer stable positioning, clear floor space, and choices for children with differing mobility. Keep scarves away from necks and faces.

Thursday | Water and containers

For toddlers who are ready, provide a shallow water exploration with large cups and adult supervision within arm's reach. Infants can explore a damp washcloth with an adult.

Teaching focus: Water requires continuous, close supervision. Empty the tray immediately after use. Do not leave a child alone near water, even briefly.

Friday | Favorite discoveries

Revisit two familiar experiences and let each child show a preference by looking, reaching, moving closer, gesturing, vocalizing, or using words.

Teaching focus: Share one specific observation with families and invite them to describe safe everyday sensory experiences their child enjoys.

Observation and documentation

Write down what the child did and the context: “looked toward the sound, then shook the rattle three times.” Track preferred materials, emerging communication, duration of engagement, and cues that signal the child wants more or needs a break.

Adaptations and extensions

Repeat favorite experiences across routines, including diapering, meals, outdoor time, and book sharing. Individualize based on developmental goals and family knowledge.

Health and safety

Stay within arm's reach during water play. Inspect every material before use, avoid choking hazards and loose parts, keep materials out of mouths unless designed for safe mouthing, and follow each child's health and allergy plan.
