🧩 Inclusion & Accessibility

Practical ways for early childhood educators and specialists to make learning environments welcoming, accessible, responsive, and meaningful for every child.

BELONGING • PARTICIPATION • INDIVIDUALIZED SUPPORT

Inclusion Means More Than Being Present

Children should have meaningful opportunities to participate, communicate, build relationships, explore, and learn. Inclusion starts with understanding each child’s strengths and needs, then adapting the environment and adult support to help that child take part.

For teachers and specialists: Use these ideas as planning and collaboration supports, not as a substitute for a child’s IFSP, IEP, professional evaluation, family input, program procedures, or applicable requirements. Coordinate adaptations with the family and the child’s support team.
INCLUSION PATHWAYS

Choose a Starting Point

Explore the area that best matches the child’s participation needs or your classroom planning question.

♿ Access & Participation

Identify and reduce barriers that make routines, play, group activities, or classroom materials difficult to access.

  • Accessible room arrangement and materials
  • Adapted activities and participation options
  • Mobility, positioning, and safe access coordination

💬 Communication Supports

Make it easier for children to understand what is happening and express choices, needs, and ideas.

  • Picture cues and visual schedules
  • Choice boards and first/then supports
  • Wait time, modeling, gestures, and communication tools

🎧 Sensory Needs & Regulation

Notice how sound, light, movement, touch, and transitions affect participation and comfort.

  • Predictable transitions and warnings
  • Quiet options and sensory-aware spaces
  • Movement opportunities and co-regulation

🧠 Individualized Adaptations

Adjust the task, materials, environment, or adult support while preserving a child’s agency and opportunity to learn.

  • Break tasks into manageable steps
  • Offer different ways to participate
  • Use strengths, interests, and observed progress

🤝 Team Collaboration

Coordinate classroom strategies with families, inclusion coordinators, special educators, therapists, and other relevant specialists.

  • Share objective observations
  • Agree on consistent strategies and roles
  • Review what helps and revise together

📝 Observation & Review

Document participation and barriers in context, without labeling a child or assuming intent.

  • Record what happened and the setting
  • Note supports tried and child response
  • Identify questions for the support team
A useful planning question: “What is making participation harder right now, and what can we change about the environment, activity, communication, or support?” Start with observable information and work collaboratively rather than expecting the child to manage every barrier alone.
