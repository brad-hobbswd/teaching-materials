POSITIVE DISCIPLINE • TEACHING SKILLS

Discipline That Teaches, Not Shames

Young children are still learning communication, impulse control, flexibility, and emotional regulation. Respond to challenging behavior with safety, connection, clear limits, and instruction. The goal is to teach the skills children need, not to label a child as bad.

1. Prevent and Prepare

Use predictable routines, visual cues, transition warnings, accessible materials, and enough space to reduce avoidable frustration.

2. Connect and Co-Regulate

Stay calm, get to the child's level when appropriate, acknowledge the feeling, and help the child return to a safe, ready-to-learn state.

3. Teach the Replacement Skill

Model and practice what to do instead: ask for a turn, request help or a break, use words or communication supports, and solve problems with support.

4. Observe the Pattern

Record what happened before and after the behavior, the context, and which supports helped. Look for communication, sensory, relationship, and environmental needs without assuming intent.

5. Partner With Families

Start with the child's strengths, listen to family knowledge, agree on consistent supports, and review progress together. Involve program specialists when additional support is needed.

6. Individualize Support

When a pattern persists or affects safety and participation, collaborate with families and appropriate program professionals on a written support plan and a review date.

Important: Avoid humiliation, threats, physical punishment, or consequences that exclude a child from learning as a behavior-management shortcut. Follow your program's policies and applicable requirements, and seek qualified support when safety or developmental concerns persist.
