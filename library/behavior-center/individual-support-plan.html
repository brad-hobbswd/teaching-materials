🌱 Individualized Positive Behavior Support Plan

A practical form for observing patterns, planning supportive responses, teaching new skills, and reviewing progress with families and the teaching team.

Back to Behavior Library
Use strengths based, objective language. Describe what you can see and hear rather than labeling a child. This tool supports classroom planning and is not a diagnostic instrument. Follow program procedures and consult the appropriate specialist or supervisor when additional support is needed.

1. Child and Planning Team

2. Strengths, Interests, and What Works

Start with what the child enjoys, communicates well, and already does successfully.

3. Describe the Concern Objectively

Record observable actions and words. Avoid assumptions about intent or character.

4. ABC Observation: Before, Behavior, After

5. Possible Purpose and Missing Skills

Use observations to form a tentative hypothesis, not a diagnosis. Gather more evidence when the pattern is unclear.

6. Proactive Supports and Environment

Select and describe supports to try before the behavior occurs.

7. Teach and Reinforce Replacement Skills

8. Safety and Dignity

Do not use this form to authorize punishment, humiliation, exclusion, or unsafe restraint. Follow applicable law, program policy, and required supervision procedures.

9. Family Partnership and Team Communication

10. Progress Review and Next Steps

Planning reminder: gather information across multiple observations, use culturally and linguistically responsive practices, protect confidentiality, and partner with families. Seek support from your program's disability, mental health, behavior, or early intervention specialists when appropriate.
