PLAY IS A WAY OF LEARNING. Curiosity turns experience into discovery.
03
START HERE

Play gives children a way to make meaning.

Young children learn by interacting with people, materials, ideas, and the world around them.

Through play, children can experiment, communicate, imagine, construct, negotiate, solve problems, test ideas, and make connections.

The teacher's role is not to take over the play. It is to create meaningful opportunities, observe what children are discovering, and intentionally extend learning.

FIVE PRINCIPLES

Make Play Purposeful

Purposeful play does not mean controlling every moment. It means creating environments and experiences where meaningful learning can emerge.

01
🔎

Start With Curiosity

Children's questions, interests, observations, and discoveries can provide powerful starting points for meaningful learning experiences.

  • Observe what captures children's attention.
  • Follow emerging interests.
  • Encourage children to ask questions.
02
👐

Give Children Something to Do

Hands on learning allows children to interact directly with materials and ideas rather than simply receiving information.

  • Provide materials children can manipulate.
  • Offer opportunities to build and create.
  • Encourage experimentation.
03
💭

Protect Imagination

Children need opportunities to develop ideas without always being given a single correct way to complete an activity.

  • Use open ended materials.
  • Accept multiple solutions.
  • Let children's ideas influence the experience.
04
🧠

Extend Thinking

Teachers can deepen learning by asking thoughtful questions, introducing new vocabulary, adding materials, and connecting play to children's ideas.

  • Ask open ended questions.
  • Introduce new concepts naturally.
  • Encourage children to explain their thinking.
05
🌱

Let Learning Grow

Meaningful play can continue across days and experiences as children revisit ideas, refine skills, and develop increasingly complex thinking.

  • Return to children's ideas.
  • Add complexity gradually.
  • Document learning over time.
LOOK AT THE PLAY

Different Play Experiences Create Different Opportunities

A strong play based classroom offers children many ways to explore ideas, interact with others, practice skills, and express what they know.

🏠
DRAMATIC PLAY

Imagine and Represent

Children explore roles, relationships, language, social situations, and real world experiences through pretend play.

🧱
CONSTRUCTIVE PLAY

Build and Solve

Blocks, loose parts, and construction materials encourage planning, spatial reasoning, persistence, and problem solving.

🎨
CREATIVE PLAY

Create and Express

Art, music, movement, and creative materials allow children to communicate ideas and explore possibilities.

🔬
INVESTIGATIVE PLAY

Question and Discover

Children observe, compare, predict, experiment, and investigate the properties of materials and the world around them.

🤝
SOCIAL PLAY

Connect and Cooperate

Shared play creates opportunities for children to communicate, negotiate, solve conflicts, take turns, collaborate, and develop relationships.

🌿
SENSORY PLAY

Explore Through the Senses

Sensory experiences invite children to investigate texture, sound, movement, weight, temperature, materials, and cause and effect.

THE TEACHER ROLE

Be present without taking over the play.

Intentional teaching and child initiated play can work together. Teachers create the conditions for learning, observe children's ideas, and know when to step in with a question, material, connection, or new challenge.

Sometimes the most meaningful teaching move is simply to watch carefully and listen.

TEACHER MOVES Try these during play:
01
Observe

Watch what children are attempting.

02
Listen

Listen for children's theories and ideas.

03
Ask

Use questions that encourage thinking.

04
Extend

Add materials or ideas that deepen learning.

05
Step Back

Give children room to continue independently.

EXTENDING LEARNING

Ask questions that open the door to thinking.

Instead of directing children toward one expected answer, use language that encourages observation, prediction, explanation, and problem solving.

EXPLORE “What do you notice?”
PREDICT “What do you think will happen?”
EXPLAIN “Tell me about what you made.”
PROBLEM SOLVE “What could we try next?”
CONNECT “What does this remind you of?”
THE TEACHER LENS

Look for the learning inside the play.

A child building a tower may be exploring balance and spatial relationships. A child pretending to run a restaurant may be developing language, social skills, mathematics, and problem solving.

When teachers look closely, ordinary play experiences can reveal extraordinary learning.

OBSERVATION GUIDE Ask yourself:
01

What is the child trying to understand?

02

What skills is the child practicing?

03

What language or ideas are emerging?

04

What could extend this learning?

05

What does the child show they already know?

✦
TEACHER REFLECTION

What is the play telling you?

Think about a recent play experience in your classroom. What were children interested in? What did they attempt? What problems did they solve? What could you add or change to help the learning continue?

One observation What did children do or say?
One discovery What learning did you notice?
One next step How could you extend the play?
