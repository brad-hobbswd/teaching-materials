01
START HERE

What children experience in a classroom begins with the environment.

A thoughtfully designed learning environment communicates powerful messages to children before a teacher says a word.

It can communicate that children are capable, that their ideas matter, that materials are available for exploration, and that the classroom belongs to everyone.

When the environment is intentional, the space becomes an active partner in teaching and learning.

FIVE PRINCIPLES

Design the Environment With Intention

Every arrangement, material, pathway, display, and learning area can serve a purpose.

01
👀

Make Learning Visible

Children should be able to see evidence that their thinking, creativity, questions, and discoveries are valued.

  • Display children's work thoughtfully.
  • Include photographs of learning experiences.
  • Preserve children's words and ideas.
02
🌱

Encourage Independence

Children learn through doing. Materials and classroom systems should allow children to participate as independently as possible.

  • Place frequently used materials within reach.
  • Use clear visual organization.
  • Create predictable classroom routines.
03
🧩

Invite Exploration

Learning spaces should encourage children to investigate, manipulate, experiment, create, and ask questions.

  • Provide open ended materials.
  • Create opportunities for discovery.
  • Rotate materials based on children's interests.
04
❤️

Create Belonging

Children need to recognize themselves, their families, their experiences, and their identities within the classroom.

  • Include diverse books and materials.
  • Represent children's families.
  • Create spaces where every child feels welcome.
05
💡

Support Creativity

Children need space to imagine, construct, represent ideas, solve problems, and express themselves in different ways.

  • Offer multiple ways to create.
  • Provide flexible materials.
  • Allow children's ideas to influence the space.
LOOK AT THE SPACE

Every Area Can Have a Purpose

Think beyond decoration. Consider what children can do, discover, practice, and communicate within each area.

📚
LANGUAGE & LITERACY

Spaces That Invite Conversation

Create comfortable opportunities for children to explore books, storytelling, writing, conversation, and communication.

🧱
BLOCKS & CONSTRUCTION

Build Ideas

Provide materials that encourage planning, problem solving, cooperation, spatial reasoning, and creativity.

🔬
STEM & SCIENCE

Make Room for Questions

Invite children to observe, investigate, test ideas, compare, predict, and discover.

🎭
DRAMATIC PLAY

Imagine Possibilities

Give children space to represent real experiences, explore roles, communicate, and build social understanding.

🎨
CREATIVE EXPRESSION

Create Without a Single Right Answer

Offer art, music, movement, sensory, and creative materials that allow children to communicate ideas in their own ways.

THE TEACHER LENS

Before changing the classroom, observe it.

Spend time watching how children actually use the space. Their movement, choices, interactions, and questions can reveal what is working and what needs to change.

The strongest classroom environments are responsive. They change as children grow, interests develop, and new learning emerges.

OBSERVATION GUIDE Ask yourself:
01

Can children access the materials they need?

02

Does the environment encourage independence?

03

Where do children naturally gather?

04

Which areas invite the most exploration?

05

What does the environment communicate about belonging?

A SIMPLE CLASSROOM RESET

You Do Not Have to Redesign Everything at Once.

Start with one area. Observe it. Ask what children need. Make one intentional change. Then watch what happens.

01

Observe

Watch how children use the space without immediately changing it.

02

Ask

Consider what the space communicates and what children may need.

03

Adjust

Make one purposeful change that supports children's learning.

04

Revisit

Observe again and allow the environment to evolve with the children.

TEACHER REFLECTION

What is your classroom telling children?

Take a few minutes to walk through your classroom from a child's perspective. What do you notice first? What can children reach? Where can they make choices? Where can they create? Where can they be quiet? Where do they see themselves?
