START HERE

Documentation helps us see what learning looks like.

Young children demonstrate learning in many different ways. They may communicate through words, movement, construction, drawing, relationships, problem solving, or play.

Documentation gives teachers a way to capture these moments and look more closely at the development taking place over time.

Strong documentation is not simply paperwork. It is evidence that can inform teaching, communicate with families, and help teachers understand each child more deeply.

FOUR WAYS TO DOCUMENT

Capture the Learning Journey

Different forms of documentation provide different windows into children's development. Use multiple sources of evidence to build a fuller picture of each child.

01
👀
OBSERVATIONS

Notice What Children Do

Careful observation allows teachers to capture children's actions, language, interactions, interests, strategies, and emerging skills.

  • Record what you actually see and hear.
  • Include meaningful details.
  • Look for patterns across experiences.
02
📖
LEARNING STORIES

Tell the Story of Learning

Learning stories bring together observations, interpretation, photographs, children's words, and teacher reflection to tell a meaningful story about learning.

  • Describe the experience.
  • Highlight the learning.
  • Consider possible next steps.
03
🗂️
PORTFOLIOS

Show Growth Over Time

Portfolios bring together meaningful samples of children's work and documentation so teachers, children, and families can see development across time.

  • Select meaningful work samples.
  • Include children's voices.
  • Show progress rather than simply collecting papers.
04
🌱
AUTHENTIC ASSESSMENT

Understand Learning in Context

Authentic assessment looks at what children know and can do within meaningful everyday experiences rather than relying only on isolated tasks.

  • Gather evidence during real experiences.
  • Consider the whole child.
  • Use evidence to inform instruction.
THE DOCUMENTATION CYCLE

Observe. Interpret. Respond.

Documentation becomes most useful when it leads back into teaching. The goal is not simply to collect evidence. The goal is to understand what children need next.

01
👁️

Observe

Pay attention to what children say, do, create, explore, and attempt.

→
02
📝

Use what you learned to plan meaningful next steps and teaching experiences.

LOOK CLOSER

What should you document?

Meaningful documentation does not require recording every moment. Focus on experiences that reveal something important about the child's development, thinking, interests, relationships, or learning process.

01
Language

What children communicate, explain, question, and describe.

02
Problem Solving

How children approach challenges, test ideas, and find solutions.

03
Social Development

How children interact, cooperate, negotiate, and build relationships.

04
Persistence

How children respond to difficulty, mistakes, and new challenges.

05
Interests

What captures children's attention and motivates them to explore.

06
Growth

Changes in skills, understanding, confidence, independence, and participation.

THE TEACHER'S ROLE

Documentation should inform what happens next.

Documentation becomes powerful when teachers use it to make decisions. What did the child demonstrate? What are they ready to explore? What support might help them move forward?

The evidence you collect should help you understand children and make intentional choices about the environment, interactions, materials, and experiences you provide.

AFTER THE OBSERVATION Ask yourself:
01
What did I notice?

Identify the important evidence.

02
What does it mean?

Think about the learning behind the action.

03
What interests the child?

Look for motivation and emerging interests.

04
What comes next?

Identify a meaningful next learning opportunity.

05
Who should know?

Consider how documentation can support family communication.

QUALITY DOCUMENTATION

Specific evidence is stronger than assumptions.

Strong documentation describes what happened before interpreting what it means.

LESS HELPFUL
×

“Maria did a great job with blocks.”

This statement gives little information about what Maria actually did.

MORE USEFUL
✓

“Maria placed three blocks across the opening and adjusted the middle block when the structure became unstable.”

Specific evidence gives the teacher a clearer picture of the child's thinking, strategy, and emerging skills.

👨‍👩‍👧
CONNECT WITH FAMILIES

Documentation can bring families into learning.

Families see parts of their child's learning that teachers may not see, while teachers observe experiences that families may miss. Sharing meaningful documentation creates opportunities for both perspectives to inform the child's learning story.

Share Celebrate meaningful moments and growth.
Invite Ask families what they notice at home.
Connect Look for relationships between home and school.

TEACHER REFLECTION

What is the documentation telling you?

Choose one child and review a recent observation, work sample, photograph, or learning story. Look beyond what happened and consider what the evidence tells you about the child's learning, interests, strengths, and possible next steps.

One interpretation What might the evidence mean?
