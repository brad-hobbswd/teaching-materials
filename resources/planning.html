INTENTIONAL PLANNING

Planning Is More Than Filling in Boxes

A strong lesson plan should help a teacher answer one important question: What am I intentionally helping children learn?

Planning becomes powerful when teachers use children's interests, observations, developmental needs, family knowledge, curriculum goals, and classroom experiences to make thoughtful decisions about what happens next.

These templates are designed to support that process without replacing professional judgment. Use them as a framework, then make them your own.

01

Observe

Notice what children are doing, saying, exploring, and attempting.

02

Plan

Use observations and goals to create meaningful learning experiences.

03

Teach

Provide intentional interactions, materials, questions, and support.

04

Reflect

Consider what children learned and determine where learning goes next.

QUICK PLANNING GUIDE

A Simple Planning Cycle

Use this cycle when creating or reviewing a lesson plan.

1

What do I know?

Begin with observations, interests, developmental information, family input, and previous learning.

2

What should children experience?

Identify the learning goal and decide what materials, experiences, interactions, and environments will support it.

3

What will I do?

Plan the questions, modeling, scaffolding, vocabulary, and intentional teaching strategies you will use.

4

What happened?

Record what children demonstrated and use that information to determine the next meaningful learning opportunity.
