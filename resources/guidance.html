Positive Guidance

Guidance begins with connection, not correction.

Build respectful relationships while encouraging self regulation, cooperation, empathy, and positive behavior.

02
START HERE

Behavior is communication, and guidance is teaching.

Young children are still developing the ability to understand emotions, communicate needs, solve problems, manage impulses, and navigate relationships.

Positive guidance recognizes that these abilities develop over time and through meaningful relationships with responsive adults.

Instead of asking only how to stop a behavior, effective guidance asks what the child is communicating and what skill the child may need help developing.

FIVE PRINCIPLES

Guide With Connection and Intention

Positive guidance is not about ignoring challenging behavior. It is about responding in ways that teach, protect relationships, and support development.

01
❤️

Build Respectful Relationships

Children are more able to accept guidance when they experience consistent, responsive, and respectful relationships with the adults around them.

  • Listen before responding.
  • Learn each child's interests and needs.
  • Maintain a calm and respectful presence.
02
🌱

Teach Self Regulation

Self regulation develops gradually. Children need adults who help them recognize emotions, practice calming strategies, and understand what they can do when feelings become strong.

  • Name emotions without judgment.
  • Model calming strategies.
  • Provide time and space to regulate.
03
🤝

Teach Cooperation

Cooperation is a developing social skill. Children need repeated opportunities to practice sharing space, solving problems, taking turns, and working with others.

  • Give children meaningful choices.
  • Model collaborative problem solving.
  • Practice turn taking.
04
🌎

Develop Empathy

Children learn empathy through relationships, modeling, conversation, and opportunities to notice how their actions affect other people.

  • Talk about feelings.
  • Model caring responses.
  • Help children consider another perspective.
05
💡

Teach Positive Behavior

Children need clear expectations and opportunities to practice appropriate ways to communicate, participate, and respond.

  • State expectations clearly.
  • Model what you want children to do.
  • Notice and reinforce positive choices.
GUIDANCE IN PRACTICE

Respond to the Need Behind the Behavior

When a challenging moment happens, pause before reacting. Consider what the child may be communicating and what support could help the child move forward.

01
PAUSE

Regulate Yourself First

A calm adult can help create a calmer interaction. Take a moment before responding.

02
OBSERVE

Look at What Happened

Consider what happened before the behavior, what the child did, and what happened afterward.

03
CONNECT

Respond to the Child

Acknowledge the child's experience while maintaining appropriate boundaries.

04
TEACH

Practice the Skill

Help the child learn another way to communicate, solve the problem, or respond.

THE LANGUAGE OF GUIDANCE

Words can either escalate the moment or create a pathway forward.

The language teachers use during challenging moments can communicate respect while still maintaining clear boundaries.

INSTEAD OF “Stop that.”
→
TRY “I won't let you hurt someone. Let's find another way.”
INSTEAD OF “You're not listening.”
→
TRY “I need your attention. Let's try that again.”
INSTEAD OF “You should know better.”
→
TRY “Let's practice what you can do next.”
THE TEACHER LENS

Before asking, “How do I stop this?” ask, “What does this child need?”

Challenging behavior can be difficult, especially when it happens repeatedly. Reflection helps teachers move beyond the immediate behavior and consider the larger context.

The goal is not to remove every difficult moment. The goal is to help children develop the skills they need to navigate those moments successfully.

REFLECTION GUIDE Ask yourself:
01

What happened immediately before the behavior?

02

What might the child be communicating?

03

Does the child understand the expectation?

04

What skill does the child need to practice?

05

How can I respond while preserving connection?

♥
TEACHER REFLECTION

Guidance is an opportunity to teach, not simply correct.

Think about a recent challenging interaction. What happened? How did you respond? What might the child have needed in that moment? What skill could you help the child practice next time?

One strength What did you handle well?
One opportunity What could you approach differently?
One next step What skill can you help children practice?
