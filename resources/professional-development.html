TEACHER LEADERSHIP

Leadership Begins With What You Do Every Day

Leadership in early childhood does not always come with a title. A teacher leads when they create a welcoming classroom, support a colleague, advocate for children, model professionalism, solve problems, share knowledge, and contribute to a stronger learning community.

Professional leadership grows when educators recognize that their everyday choices influence children, families, colleagues, and the larger program.

01

Lead Yourself

Develop self awareness, reliability, professionalism, and a commitment to continued learning.

02

Lead the Classroom

Create environments where children feel safe, capable, respected, challenged, and supported.

03

Lead With Others

Collaborate, communicate, listen, share ideas, and contribute to professional teams.

04

Lead Change

Use evidence, reflection, and professional courage to improve practice.

TEACHING PRACTICE

Small Changes Can Strengthen Practice

Professional growth often begins with one intentional change in the classroom.

01

Observe Before Intervening

Give yourself time to understand what children are communicating through their actions before deciding how to respond.

02

Ask Better Questions

Use questions that invite children to explain, predict, compare, reason, describe, and reflect.

03

Extend Children's Thinking

Take what children already know and add a question, material, challenge, or perspective that moves learning forward.

04

Reflect on the Environment

Ask whether the classroom environment invites independence, exploration, relationships, communication, and meaningful learning.

05

Make Learning Visible

Document meaningful observations and use them to communicate learning with colleagues and families.

06

Try, Reflect, Adjust

Professional growth happens when educators test ideas, examine results, and make thoughtful adjustments.
