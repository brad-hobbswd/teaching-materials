START WITH BELONGING

Inclusion begins with knowing every child matters.

An inclusive classroom is more than a room where every child is allowed to participate. It is a learning community intentionally designed so children can see themselves, contribute their ideas, develop relationships, and experience genuine belonging.

Teachers create inclusion through the environment, materials, relationships, expectations, routines, communication, and opportunities they provide.

When children experience belonging, they are better positioned to participate, explore, communicate, take risks, and learn alongside others.

FIVE FOUNDATIONS

Build Inclusion Into Everyday Practice

Inclusion is not one activity or one lesson. It is reflected in the decisions teachers make throughout the entire day.

01
🪞
REPRESENTATION

Help Children See Themselves

Provide books, photographs, dramatic play materials, artwork, music, and classroom displays that reflect the children and families in your learning community.

Ask: Who sees themselves reflected here?
02
♿
ACCESS

Make Participation Possible

Consider how children move, communicate, interact with materials, follow routines, and participate in experiences. Adjust the environment when barriers appear.

Ask: What could make participation easier?
03
🤝
RELATIONSHIPS

Build a Community of Belonging

Develop warm, respectful relationships with children and families while creating opportunities for children to connect, cooperate, listen, and learn from one another.

Ask: How does each child know they belong?
04
💬
VOICE

Make Space for Every Child's Ideas

Listen carefully to children's questions, choices, preferences, communication, and perspectives. Give children meaningful ways to influence their experiences.

Ask: Whose voice am I hearing?
05
🌱
FLEXIBILITY

Respond to Individual Needs

Children do not all learn, communicate, regulate, or participate in the same way. Responsive teaching allows educators to adjust expectations, materials, support, and strategies when needed.

Ask: What does this child need right now?
THE ENVIRONMENT SPEAKS

What does your classroom communicate?

Children receive messages from the classroom before a teacher says a single word. The materials available, pictures displayed, pathways provided, routines established, and spaces created all communicate who belongs and how children are expected to participate.

An inclusive environment intentionally removes unnecessary barriers and creates multiple ways for children to enter, participate, communicate, explore, and contribute.

CLASSROOM CHECK Look around your room.
✓
Children can access materials.

Materials are reachable and organized so children can participate independently.

✓
Children see themselves represented.

Classroom materials reflect children, families, cultures, and communities.

✓
Multiple ways to participate exist.

Children have varied ways to communicate, explore, create, and demonstrate learning.

✓
Spaces support different needs.

The classroom includes opportunities for movement, quiet, collaboration, and focused play.

FAMILIES ARE PART OF THE COMMUNITY

Inclusion extends beyond the classroom.

Strong partnerships with families help teachers understand children's experiences, strengths, interests, communication, culture, routines, and individual needs.

👂

Listen

Invite families to share what they know about their child.

💛

Respect

Honor family perspectives, experiences, traditions, and priorities.

🔗

Connect

Look for meaningful connections between home experiences and classroom learning.

🌎

Include

Create genuine opportunities for families to contribute to the classroom community.

🌎
EVERY CHILD

Inclusion is an everyday practice, not a special occasion.

The strongest inclusive classrooms do not wait for a special event to celebrate differences. They build belonging into everyday routines, conversations, materials, relationships, and learning experiences.

Inclusion means recognizing that each child brings strengths, experiences, perspectives, communication styles, interests, and needs that deserve to be understood and supported.

Every child should be able to say: “I belong here.”
✦
TEACHER REFLECTION

Who is included, and who might still be waiting?

Take a quiet look at one part of your classroom. Consider the materials, routines, interactions, expectations, and opportunities available to children. Ask yourself whether every child has a meaningful way to participate and belong.

Look What does the environment communicate?
Listen Whose voices are being heard?
Change What is one barrier you can remove?
