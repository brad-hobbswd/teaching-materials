October 2026 Teaching Resource

Exploring Fall, Community, Senses, and Imaginative Play

Purpose: Use these ideas as flexible planning support. Follow children's interests, provide meaningful choices, and adapt experiences for individual development, culture, communication, and accessibility.

Week 1: Fall Changes and Nature

  • Science: Observe leaves, weather, light, temperature, and changes in the outdoor environment.
  • Math: Sort natural materials by color, size, shape, or texture.
  • Language: Build descriptive vocabulary such as crunchy, smooth, dry, wet, bright, and windy.
  • Art: Create leaf rubbings, collages, or nature prints.

Week 2: Community Helpers

  • Dramatic play: Transform a play area into a clinic, store, kitchen, construction site, or community service setting.
  • Language: Talk about what familiar community helpers do and how they help people.
  • Math: Count, sort, and compare pretend tools, uniforms, signs, or supplies.
  • Social emotional: Explore helping, cooperation, and caring for others.

Week 3: Pumpkins, Apples, and the Five Senses

  • Science: Investigate pumpkins or apples using sight, smell, touch, and taste when appropriate.
  • Math: Compare size, count seeds, and explore full, empty, more, and less.
  • Art: Paint or print with pumpkin and apple shapes using washable materials.
  • Language: Encourage children to describe observations and make predictions.

Week 4: Imagination, Costumes, and Storytelling

  • Dramatic play: Provide open-ended props for children to invent characters and stories.
  • Literacy: Retell familiar stories using puppets, props, pictures, or children's own words.
  • Movement: Move like animals, characters, or objects from children's stories.
  • Social emotional: Practice taking turns, negotiating roles, and respecting others' ideas.

Family Connection

Invite families to take a short walk together and notice one sign of fall. Encourage children to describe what they see, hear, smell, or feel and share the observation with the class.

Materials

Leaves, natural objects, pumpkins or apples, baskets, sorting containers, washable art materials, dramatic play props, books, puppets, rhythm instruments, and sensory materials appropriate for the children.

Teacher Observation

Notice children's descriptive language, curiosity, classification skills, cooperative play, storytelling, problem solving, and ability to communicate ideas.

Adaptation

Offer visual supports, sensory alternatives, adaptive tools, multiple communication methods, and quieter participation options. Avoid requiring children to participate in any seasonal activity they find uncomfortable.

Planning reminder: This resource is a planning companion, not a script. Follow children's questions and interests, document authentic learning, and adjust experiences to the children in your classroom.
