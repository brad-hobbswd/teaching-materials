Developmental Approach

Follow the Child, Not Just the Curriculum

Infants & Toddlers

Build the Literacy Foundation

Early Head Start children do not need formal alphabet instruction as a primary learning goal. Responsive conversation, shared books, songs, gestures, storytelling, picture exploration, and meaningful print experiences provide rich early literacy foundations.

Preschool

Explore Letters Through Play

Preschool children can begin exploring letters when they show interest and developmental readiness. Matching, noticing letters in names, songs, games, and playful literacy centers can make letter learning meaningful.

Pre-K & Kindergarten

Make Learning More Intentional

Older preschool and kindergarten children may benefit from more intentional opportunities to recognize letters, compare uppercase and lowercase forms, explore beginning sounds, and use alphabet materials during literacy learning.

Teacher Tips

Make Your Alphabet Cards Last All Year

Print on heavy cardstock, laminate for durability, organize by letter in labeled containers, and use hook and loop dots or magnetic strips to create interactive classroom displays.

  • Laminate for repeated use
  • Store by alphabet sequence
  • Use during morning meeting
  • Rotate into literacy centers
  • Send home for family practice
