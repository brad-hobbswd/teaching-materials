Follow the Child's Development

Beginning sound activities are most appropriate when children are developmentally ready to explore sounds in words. For younger children, especially infants, toddlers, and younger preschoolers, teachers can build the foundation through talking, singing, rhyming, picture naming, and playful sound exploration without expecting formal letter or phonics mastery.

Teaching Ideas

Make Sound Learning Playful

Beginning sound learning does not have to feel like a worksheet. Use pictures, conversation, movement, songs, and games to help children listen closely to the sounds in words.

01

Say It Slowly

Say a familiar word clearly and invite children to listen to the sound they hear first.

02

Use Pictures

Picture cards provide a concrete way for children to compare words and notice similarities in sounds.

03

Sort Together

Begin with only two sounds and let children physically move pictures into matching groups.

04

Follow Their Interest

Use familiar names, animals, classroom objects, and children's interests to make sound exploration meaningful.
